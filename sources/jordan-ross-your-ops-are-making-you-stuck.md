# Your Ops are Making You Stuck.

## Post text

The average agency operation sucks.

Agency owners typically go out on their own because they're talented marketers, not talented operators.

The result: the whole industry sucks at scaling.

The solution: build your business like amazon.

## Full article text

The reason your marketing agency is stuck is that you built it like an agency.

Instead, you need to build it like an Amazon warehouse operation.

I can say that because I had the pleasure of being professionally hazed by Amazon for four years. I managed over 2,500 employees collectively during my time at the company as an operations leader, and I brought all of that experience with me when I started 8 Figure Agency in 2019.

Since then, we've worked with over 1,200 agencies. I've seen every scenario an agency can possibly go through, and I can tell you: throw the agency operating model out the fucking window.

Here's how to think about your business from an Amazon operations perspective instead. And in a world of AI, scaling an agency is far easier now than it has ever been.

If you want to build like Amazon, follow these steps in this order.

## 1. Process mapping

If your processes live in someone's head, you are not running a good business.

Amazon has every process mapped and every flow defined. That's the foundation for everything that follows.

## 2. Benchmarking

Once your processes are defined, you can benchmark them.

At Amazon, every part of the operation has a statistical benchmark.

Hourly, daily, monthly. Every component has a measured data point: how did it perform versus how it was supposed to perform?

At the end of every day, when a team member falls short on a line item of the warehouse P&L or on a KPI, they conduct a bridge: we were supposed to be here but we landed here, here's why, here's what we're going to do differently, here's what we learned.

That reporting cadence and visibility creates social pressure to perform. If you keep missing, you don't get promoted and you don't get a raise, because the benchmarks are defined and they're public.

The reporting is automated.

It surfaces the data hourly, daily, weekly, and monthly, and sends to the right distribution list by email.

The annual and semi-annual performance reviews aggregate that data into each individual's dashboard, and the system tells you whether someone is up for promotion based on whether they hit their benchmarks.

Think about that for your agency.

The probability that you do anything like it is about 0.001.

Everything at Amazon is a science.

## 3. Meeting rhythm

At Amazon, meetings are bottom-up.

When a manager running a segment of a department, or an operations leader running an entire department, meets with their superiors, they show up ready to present the data.

If you're executing, it's a footnote:

"Here's my agenda for today. I'm going to review performance, review prior actions, and review next steps.

Of the 128 KPIs in our operation, today we executed on 95%. There are six I want to review. Root-causing them, we're missing because of X, and our plan to get them back to standard is Y.

Yesterday we were missing on the following…..

We implemented yesterday's actions, and here's what we learned.

Overall, the business is in good operational health. We're going to keep executing, and here are the new actions we're implementing."

Every meeting that reviews business data, from an entry-level manager up to the executive team, runs like this.

## 4. Constraints

There are two strategies you can use to grow your business: constraint theory and the Pareto principle.

To earn the right to focus on high-leverage strategy, you need to execute on constraints first. This took me literally a decade to learn.

At Amazon, operators and managers run the business on constraints.

A warehouse has a targeted throughput: a desired outcome for the week, the month, and the quarter.

In your business, your throughput is your profit goal. If you're doing $100K a month, you probably want $200K. If you're doing $500K, you probably want $1M.

Constraint theory says an operation can only move as fast as its slowest part.

That part is the constraint. If you run a fully defined operation on constraint theory, you will get there. No question.

In an agency there are six constraints to track:

1. Calls booked

1. Deals closed, and at what price point

1. Capacity to onboard and fulfill

1. Lifetime value

1. Cash flow

1. Time (if your operation is a shit show, you'll be fighting fires all day and you won't have any)

To run the operation well, every week and every month your team must have a constraint they're solving.

Example: you have high churn.

Every time you onboard more than five clients in a month, four of them churn within 60 days. The business can't get bigger because you're losing clients faster than you can add them. Your constraint is churn.

So the ops team has to:

1. Identify, from a root-cause perspective, why it's happening

1. Identify a new process to build

1. Build it

1. Implement the changes

1. Sustain it in perpetuity

Do that and the constraint alleviates. Churn drops, and the business that couldn't grow past $100K a month can now grow to $200K.

Once your people can do this on your behalf, you can start removing yourself from the operation.

## 5. Stress testing

Every seven-figure agency is constrained by its founder.

Usually it's because the agency doesn't have the cash flow, resources, or time to recruit the best talent. So the founder is the best talent.

That problem can go away now faster than ever.

With AI, once you identify your constraints and rebuild them as augmented or automated processes, a team member who could only manage 15 accounts worth $75K a month can at least manage five more. Those five account, in this example, are an extra $25K a month in pure cash.

Use that cash to acquire better talent. The better talent can manage more and increase LTV. Everything positively compounds.

Once that's happening, you can run what's called a stress test.

At Amazon, we ran a stress test before every peak season: before the holidays at the end of the year, and before Prime week in the middle of summer.

We intentionally threw a massive amount of volume at the operation to see what broke. Then we aggregated and studied the breaks and built new processes so nothing broke when it was game time.

In your business, the stress test is you getting the F out of the operation.

Start by taking one week off at least every two months. Then two weeks. Then a month.

When you come back, your team will have a list of everything that broke.

From that list, you identify which processes need to be built so the business doesn't rely on you. Your team builds them, implements them, and sustains them. Do this correctly and within 12 to 24 months, you're out of the operation.

## 6. Strategy

If you're one of the few who actually gets out of the operation, you get to start working on the business.

This is where things truly change.

Think about the last time you took a real vacation from your business. You saw things differently. You had the mental space and capacity to think. And 100% of the time, you came back with ideas that, if implemented, would have changed the business.

Now imagine that all the time.

White space on an owner's calendar is the differentiating factor. It's what lets you see the patterns and strategies that disproportionately grow the business. When you're in the operation, your brain is too constrained and too stressed to see anything objectively. From a distance, you can easily spot the 20% of activities that will drive 80% of your growth.

Constraint theory gets you gradual improvement and gradual growth: 20% to 100% year over year. Removing yourself and looking at the business objectively is what gets you the radical, different strategies.

One example: my buddy Marketing Max decided to build a media company with hundreds of thousands of business owners on his newsletter. He now runs a seven-figure media business, promotes his own companies inside the newsletter, and literally has negative CAC.

That happened because he had white space on his calendar to THINK about an elite solution.

Amazing strategists have space to think.

## 7. Auditing

Once systems are implemented, they eventually drift.

Agencies with good founders scale quickly from zero to $1M, and from $1M to $2M or $3M, because the founder has very high standards.

But the first thing that goes out the window when an agency scales is auditing.

The founder gets too busy, all shit rolls uphill, they start firefighting, and they can no longer audit their sales team, their fulfillment team, or their operations team. Standards drop as a result.

With AI, you can now audit every call, every email, and every Slack message, and at the end of the day build a report that tells you whether your agency's standards are dropping. It can call out the specific people, systems, KPIs, or qualitative measures that aren't meeting the standard, and send that report to whoever needs it.

That report saves the owner a boatload of time on work they don't have time for, and makes it easy for management to go audit the specific associate who's been flagged.

Auditing opens the window to find low performance. Low performance is a result of either a people problem or a systems problem.

If it's a systems problem, the fix is straightforward: there's a barrier in front of one of your team members that needs to be removed.

If it's not a systems problem, you have a people problem. That person needs to be coached consistently, up to the point where coaching either works or that person needs to be terminated.

Do all of this in unison and you will build an AMAZING business. Period.
